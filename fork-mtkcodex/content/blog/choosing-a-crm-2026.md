A CRM is where your revenue lives: every lead, conversation, quote and follow-up. Pick the right one and your sales team moves faster with less admin. Pick the wrong one and it becomes an expensive spreadsheet nobody updates.

The first decision most businesses face is whether to adopt an established platform — HubSpot, Zoho, Salesforce and similar — or build a CRM around their own process. Here is how we help clients decide.

## Start with the process, not the software

Before comparing tools, write down how a lead actually becomes a customer in your business:

- Where do leads come from? Website forms, WhatsApp, phone calls, ads, referrals, marketplaces?
- What happens in the first hour? Who is assigned, and how?
- What are the real stages of your pipeline, and what moves a deal from one to the next?
- What documents are involved — quotes, contracts, invoices, measurements, site visits?
- What do managers need to see every Monday morning?

If your answers look like a standard B2B or B2C sales funnel, an off-the-shelf CRM will likely fit. If they involve industry-specific steps — installment schedules, custom measurements, multi-branch approvals, field visits — you may be fighting the tool from day one.

## When an off-the-shelf CRM is the right choice

Platforms like HubSpot, Zoho and Salesforce are excellent when:

- Your process is close to a standard sales pipeline.
- You want to start quickly and grow into advanced features.
- Your team benefits from a large ecosystem of integrations and training material.
- Per-user pricing fits your budget as you scale.

Most of the value comes from **configuration**: designing pipelines and properties correctly, setting up lead scoring and assignment, building follow-up sequences, connecting your website and messaging channels, and creating dashboards. A well-configured standard CRM beats a poorly configured expensive one every time.

## When a custom CRM wins

A custom CRM makes sense when:

- Your workflow is genuinely unique and central to how you make money.
- You need deep integration with your own products, databases or local payment gateways.
- Per-seat licensing becomes expensive for a large team or for giving access to partners and customers.
- You want full ownership of your data and the roadmap.

The trade-off is upfront investment and the need for ongoing maintenance — which is why we build custom CRMs on the same proven multi-tenant foundation as our SaaS products, with role-based access, audit logs and automation built in.

## Features that matter more than the logo

Whichever route you choose, these capabilities decide whether a CRM gets used:

1. **Automatic lead capture** from every channel, so nobody copies data by hand.
2. **Assignment and response alerts**, because speed to first contact drives conversion.
3. **Follow-up automation** on the channels customers actually use — in many markets that means WhatsApp, not just email.
4. **Quotes and invoices** generated from deal data.
5. **Simple dashboards** that show pipeline value, conversion by source and team activity.
6. **Mobile access** for teams in the field.

## A low-risk way to decide

Run a short discovery: map the process, list must-have integrations, estimate users over three years, and compare total cost of ownership. Often the answer is a hybrid — a standard CRM, configured well, plus a small custom service that handles the one workflow no tool supports.
